Practice Questions

15 challenging questions covering this course, designed to test the kind of thinking a real exam would demand. Attempt every question fully before you look at the model answers.

View Model Answers →
How to use this: Work through all 15 questions on your own first, in writing if possible. Then open the Model Answers page to check your understanding.

  1. A public organization has repeated performance problems but does not know whether the cause is its procedures, its organizational structure or its implementation methods, so it calls in a consultant. Using the course's own definitions of consultancy and of a management consultant, explain why the consultant should investigate the problem before making recommendations, and explain why the independence of the consultant matters to this process.

  2. A trading company needs short-term specialist advice and is deciding between two people: one is a full-time employee of an established consulting firm, and the other recently retired from a senior executive role in a related industry and now takes on part-time assignments. Using the course's descriptions of the types of consultants, explain the difference between these two types and justify, with reasons from the notes, which one better fits a short-term, part-time need.

  3. A manufacturing company operates in a niche industry with unique technical requirements and is choosing between a general management consulting firm and an industrial specialist consulting firm. Using the course's own descriptions of these two firm types, compare their approaches and justify which type of firm the company should choose and why.

  4. Consultant A personally collects all the data on a client's problem, forms an expert opinion, and hands the client a final recommendation. Consultant B instead interviews workers, presents the data back to the client, lets the client interpret it, and works with the client so the client learns from acting on the research. Using the Margulies and Raia model, identify which consultant is task-oriented and which is process-oriented, and explain, using at least three points of contrast from the model, what is different about how each approach treats the client's role and learning.

  5. During one consultancy assignment, a consultant first spends time simply gathering and reporting facts without offering any opinion, later suggests several possible alternative solutions and connects the client with outside resources that could help, and at another point runs a training session to build the client's own skills. Using Lippitt's model of consultant roles, name the specific role being performed at each of these three moments, and explain why it is useful for a consultant to be able to move between different roles like this during a single assignment.

  6. A consultant holds an advanced degree and has completed functionally oriented courses through a professional society, but has never before worked in the mining sector, and is now asked to advise a mining company. Using the course's distinction between functional knowledge and industrial knowledge, explain what kind of knowledge this consultant already has, what kind is missing, and how each type of knowledge is normally acquired according to the notes.

  7. A consultant has strong technical knowledge but ignores stakeholder concerns, struggles to work with a team, and treats ethical considerations as secondary to getting quick results. Using at least four of the course's ten behavioral skills, identify which skills this consultant is failing to demonstrate and explain, for each one, why the notes consider it important to effective consultancy practice.

  8. A client can only describe their problem vaguely as "things are not working," and once the consultant identifies the real issue, staff resist the proposed change. Using the course's list of other consultancy skills, explain which specific skills the consultant needs to use, in what order, to move from an unclear complaint to a solution that staff will accept.

  9. A firm has noticed declining customer satisfaction but has not yet worked out why, and wants to build a full plan to grow its consulting services around this issue over time. Apply the course's five-step practice consultancy development strategy, in order, to this situation, explaining what the firm should do at each step.

  10. Explain, using the course's own points on the meaning and nature of consultancy management, what makes consultancy "knowledge-based" and "professional," why the relationship between a consultant and a client is described as temporary, and why confidentiality matters given that consultancy is meant to provide independent, objective advice.

  11. An organization asks a consultant to analyze why its programs are underperforming, recommend strategic changes, and also review whether its project results are being properly tracked and assessed. Using the course's list of main functions of consultancy and its list of types of consultancy, identify which functions are involved and which type or types of consultancy this assignment falls under, with reasons.

  12. A consultancy firm is operating in a period where clients are more value-conscious and compare costs closely, where artificial intelligence and data analytics are changing how consulting work is delivered, and where the firm is finding it hard to keep skilled staff. Using the course's lists of consultancy market conditions and market challenges, analyze this situation and recommend, using only ideas already in the notes, how the firm should respond.

  13. A person wants to establish a new consultancy firm from scratch. Using the course's procedure for establishing a consultancy firm, list the steps in the correct order and explain, for this person's situation, what each step involves.

  14. During an M&E assignment, a consultant must collect accurate data from confused stakeholders, manage stakeholder expectations that differ from what the project can deliver, and later present the findings clearly. Using the course's interpersonal communication skills and its table on communication skills in M&E consulting, identify which specific skills are needed for each of these three tasks and explain what each skill contributes.

  15. A newly registered consultancy firm in Tanzania wants to become fully tax compliant. Using the course's Tanzania-specific tax notes, explain the steps the firm should take, including registration and the different returns it must file, and then use the course's list of reasons why tax compliance matters to justify why this effort is worthwhile for the firm.
