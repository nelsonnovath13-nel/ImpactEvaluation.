Practice Questions

15 challenging questions covering the whole course (L1, L2 and L3), designed to test the kind of thinking a real exam would demand. Attempt every question fully before you look at the model answers.

How to use this: Work through all 15 questions on your own first, in writing if possible. Then open the Model Answers page to check your understanding.

  1. Using the source definitions, explain the difference between "evaluation" in general and "impact evaluation" specifically. Why does impact evaluation require a counterfactual, and what problem would arise if an evaluator simply looked at outcomes measured after a project ended, without considering the counterfactual?

  2. A vocational training project trained young people in tailoring. Six months later, 70% of those trained had found jobs. Around the same time, the project's design turned out to closely match the priorities of a newly launched national youth-employment policy. Using the OECD DAC criteria definitions and their key questions, identify which two criteria are most clearly demonstrated in this scenario and explain why, using the source's own definitions and example wording for each.

  3. Compare mid-term/interim evaluation and ex post evaluation as described in the source's table: when does each happen, and what is its main focus? Why might a donor agency want to commission both types on the same project rather than only one?

  4. The source states that DAC criteria can be integrated into the LFA Monitoring and Evaluation Matrix, with each criterion linked to a level of the logical framework. Using the "Practical LFA Example" table, state which DAC criteria are linked to the Purpose level and which are linked to the Goal level, and give the example indicator listed for each level.

  5. A project team tracks the number of training workshops delivered and the budget utilization rate every quarter while the project is running. A separate team, two years after the project has closed, assesses whether the increase in women's income has lasted and whether local institutions can now sustain the programme without donor support. Identify which activity is monitoring and which is evaluation, and name the DAC criteria each one is primarily tracking according to the source's three conclusions on monitoring versus evaluation.

  6. Explain why the source describes randomized controlled trials (RCTs) as the "gold standard" for determining causal relationships. In your answer, describe the role of random assignment, the control group, and the treatment group, and explain how randomization addresses both known and unknown confounding variables.

  7. A ministry of education wants to know whether a new teaching method improves test scores. Randomly assigning individual students within the same school to the new method or the traditional method is considered impractical, so instead the ministry compares one classroom already using the new method against another classroom continuing the traditional method. (a) Name the type of design this is, according to the source. (b) Using the source's own school example, identify a specific confounding risk in this design. (c) Name the three methods the source gives for addressing confounding variables in general, and briefly explain how one of them (other than randomization) could still improve this study.

  8. The source's own RCT example asks whether a new complementary feeding practice improves the nutritional status of under-five children in Tanzania. List, in order, all nine of the source's steps for conducting an RCT, and for each step briefly state what it would involve in this specific study, drawing on the details given in the RCT example section.

  9. Using the Difference-in-Differences model Y = α + β₁(Time) + β₂(Treatment) + β₃(Time × Treatment) + ε and the school meal-plan example, explain in plain terms what each of α, β₁, β₂ and β₃ represents. State specifically which coefficient is the "DiD estimator" and explain why it — rather than β₁ or β₂ alone — captures the causal effect of the meal plan.

  10. Compare a quasi-experimental design with a non-experimental design as described in the source. Does either design involve the researcher manipulating variables or intervening with the groups being studied? Give one named example of each type from the source's tables, and explain what distinguishes them from each other.

  11. A researcher is studying whether exercise (the independent variable) improves mental health (the dependent variable), but is concerned that income level might influence both how much people are able to exercise and their mental health. Using the source's definition of a confounding variable, explain why income is a potential confounder here. Then, using the correct named method from the source, describe how a statistical approach could address this without needing random assignment.

  12. A survey team wants to estimate the school dropout rate among adolescent girls in a district. No prior estimate of this proportion exists, so, following the source's guidance, they use 50%. They want a 95% confidence level and a desired degree of accuracy of 0.05. Using the source's formula for a population greater than 10,000, calculate the required sample size step by step, in the style of the source's own worked example.

  13. Suppose the district in the previous question actually has a total adolescent-girl population of only 3,000, which is fewer than 10,000. Using the sample size you calculated in the previous question and the source's finite-population formula, calculate the adjusted required sample size. Show your working.

  14. An NGO gave a cash-transfer programme to some households, but households were not randomly assigned — they opted in based on need. The NGO wants to estimate the causal effect of the transfer on children's school attendance. Using the source's Propensity Score Matching (PSM) chain from Topic Three, explain step by step how the NGO should proceed. Name each required element (treatment variable, covariates, outcome variable), the estimation and matching approach, and the two checks that must be carried out before analyzing outcomes.

  15. The source's covariate balance table shows standardized differences before and after matching (for example, Age: 0.45 before matching and 0.05 after, with p = 0.03 before and p = 0.72 after). Explain what this pattern indicates about selection bias before matching, what threshold the source uses to judge "good balance," and why the source describes a well-balanced matched sample as creating a "pseudo-randomized comparison group." Then, using the outcome analysis results (mean difference 3.9, t(94) = 2.87, p = .005), explain what conclusion the notes draw about the treatment effect.
