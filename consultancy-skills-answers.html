  1. 1A public organization has repeated performance problems but does not know whether the cause is its procedures, its organizational structure or its implementation methods, so it calls in a consultant. Using the course's own definitions of consultancy and of a management consultant, explain why the consultant should investigate the problem before making recommendations, and explain why the independence of the consultant matters to this process.

    The notes define management consultancy as a service provided by an independent and qualified person for identifying and investigating problems concerned with policy, organization, procedures and methods, recommending appropriate action and helping to implement those recommendations. Because the organization itself does not know whether the real cause is its procedures, its structure or its implementation methods, the consultant cannot skip straight to recommending action; the definition itself places "identifying and investigating" before "recommending appropriate action," so investigation is the step that finds the true cause instead of guessing at it. Consultancy is also described as offering independent and specialized knowledge to support decision making, planning, implementation and evaluation, which again shows that understanding the problem properly comes first. Independence matters because the consultant is meant to give objective, expert advice rather than advice shaped by internal politics or pressure from any one part of the organization, and the notes warn that the title "consultant" is sometimes used by people without the necessary training, skills or personal disposition, so genuine independence and professional competence are what make the investigation and the resulting recommendations trustworthy.

  2. 2A trading company needs short-term specialist advice and is deciding between two people: one is a full-time employee of an established consulting firm, and the other recently retired from a senior executive role in a related industry and now takes on part-time assignments. Using the course's descriptions of the types of consultants, explain the difference between these two types and justify, with reasons from the notes, which one better fits a short-term, part-time need.

    The course lists three types of consultants: the full-time consultant, who works for an established firm; the professor, who may take on consultancy as a sideline; and the executive, who may be between jobs or retired and who undertakes consultancy on a part-time basis. The first person in the scenario matches the full-time consultant type, since they work for an established firm as their main occupation. The second person, who has just retired from an executive role and now takes on assignments part-time, matches the executive type exactly as described in the notes. Because the company's need is short-term and part-time rather than an ongoing engagement, the executive type is the better fit, since the notes specifically describe this type as suited to part-time work, while the full-time consultant is tied to the firm's ongoing, established practice.

  3. 3A manufacturing company operates in a niche industry with unique technical requirements and is choosing between a general management consulting firm and an industrial specialist consulting firm. Using the course's own descriptions of these two firm types, compare their approaches and justify which type of firm the company should choose and why.

    The notes describe the general management consulting firm as one that looks at a problem from an overall view of the company and uses a holistic approach, meaning it considers the whole organization rather than one narrow area. The industrial specialist firm, by contrast, is described as serving industries with unique needs requiring special help, and the notes state that a company may seek someone specializing in its own industry. Since the manufacturing company in this scenario has unique technical requirements tied specifically to its industry, the industrial specialist firm is the better justified choice, because its whole purpose according to the notes is to meet exactly this kind of industry-specific need, whereas the general management firm's strength is a broad, whole-company view that is not focused on unique technical or industry demands.

  4. 4Consultant A personally collects all the data on a client's problem, forms an expert opinion, and hands the client a final recommendation. Consultant B instead interviews workers, presents the data back to the client, lets the client interpret it, and works with the client so the client learns from acting on the research. Using the Margulies and Raia model, identify which consultant is task-oriented and which is process-oriented, and explain, using at least three points of contrast from the model, what is different about how each approach treats the client's role and learning.

    Consultant A is task-oriented and Consultant B is process-oriented, according to the Margulies and Raia model. Three contrasts from the model explain this. First, on who collects and interprets the data, the task-oriented consultant acts as expert and collects data personally and gives an expert opinion, which matches Consultant A, while the process-oriented consultant provides data and allows clients to interpret it, which matches Consultant B interviewing workers and handing data back to the client. Second, on decision-making, the task approach has the consultant make final recommendations based on the data, again matching Consultant A, while the process approach is more involved with working with clients and gathering feedback, matching Consultant B. Third, on learning, the process consultant develops client learning by having the client take action on the research and is described in the notes as acting like a teacher, which is exactly what Consultant B is doing, while the task-oriented approach keeps its concern focused on the problem itself rather than on building the client's own capability.

  5. 5During one consultancy assignment, a consultant first spends time simply gathering and reporting facts without offering any opinion, later suggests several possible alternative solutions and connects the client with outside resources that could help, and at another point runs a training session to build the client's own skills. Using Lippitt's model of consultant roles, name the specific role being performed at each of these three moments, and explain why it is useful for a consultant to be able to move between different roles like this during a single assignment.

    In Lippitt's eight-role model, gathering and reporting facts without giving an opinion is the fact-finder role. Suggesting several possible solutions and connecting the client to outside resources is the identifier of alternatives and linker to resources role. Running a training session to build the client's own skills is the trainer/educator role. It is useful for a consultant to move between these roles within one assignment because, as the notes put it, a consultant's contribution can take several forms depending on the assignment, not simply giving a final answer; different moments in an assignment call for different kinds of help, such as neutral fact gathering at one stage, options and connections at another, and skill-building at another, and Lippitt's model shows that all of these are legitimate parts of a consultant's job rather than a departure from it.

  6. 6A consultant holds an advanced degree and has completed functionally oriented courses through a professional society, but has never before worked in the mining sector, and is now asked to advise a mining company. Using the course's distinction between functional knowledge and industrial knowledge, explain what kind of knowledge this consultant already has, what kind is missing, and how each type of knowledge is normally acquired according to the notes.

    The consultant already has functional knowledge, which the notes describe as acquired through academic studies and long-term work experience, and note that some functions may also be learned through functionally oriented courses offered by professional societies such as the Society for Human Resource Management, the American Marketing Association and the Society for Information Management; the consultant's advanced degree and professional society courses fit this description exactly. What is missing is industrial knowledge, which the notes describe as acquired through years of experience in a particular industry, and since this consultant has never worked in mining, they lack the industry-specific experience that this category requires. The notes connect the two by saying functional knowledge relates to professional functions while industrial knowledge relates to experience within a specific industry, so this consultant would need to either gain mining-industry experience or work alongside someone who has it before the engagement can draw on both types of knowledge.

  7. 7A consultant has strong technical knowledge but ignores stakeholder concerns, struggles to work with a team, and treats ethical considerations as secondary to getting quick results. Using at least four of the course's ten behavioral skills, identify which skills this consultant is failing to demonstrate and explain, for each one, why the notes consider it important to effective consultancy practice.

    • Respect for self and others: the notes say consultants should explore similarities and differences to find shared understanding, and that respect enhances understanding and service and fosters harmonious relationships; ignoring stakeholder concerns shows a failure of this skill.
    • Team-working ability: the notes state that effective team-based work requires knowledge of organizational design, process management and change facilitation, and mention the Teamwork Knowledge, Skills, and Ability (KSA) test as a predictor of effective teamwork; struggling to work with a team shows this skill is missing.
    • Ability to abide principles: the notes link this skill to ethical and effective service delivery and to balancing ethical considerations, stakeholder interests and long-term outcomes; treating ethics as secondary to quick results directly contradicts this skill.
    • Ability to care for others: the notes connect caring for others to sociability and social capital, and describe balancing emotional engagement with reliable service; ignoring stakeholder concerns also suggests a lack of this caring orientation.

    Together, these gaps show that technical knowledge alone is not enough, since the notes state that professional behavior also affects consultancy practice.

  8. 8A client can only describe their problem vaguely as "things are not working," and once the consultant identifies the real issue, staff resist the proposed change. Using the course's list of other consultancy skills, explain which specific skills the consultant needs to use, in what order, to move from an unclear complaint to a solution that staff will accept.

    The consultant should first use diagnosis of the problem skills together with analytical skills to work through the vague complaint and analyze what is actually going wrong, since these skills are what allow a general statement like "things are not working" to be turned into a clear, specific problem. Once the real issue is identified, the consultant needs communication skills to explain the finding clearly to staff, and then persuasive and educational skills to help resistant staff understand and accept the proposed change rather than simply being told what to do. Finally, problem-solving skills tie the process together, since they are needed both to work out the solution itself and to guide its acceptance and use. This order, understand and diagnose, then communicate and persuade, then solve, reflects the course's point that together these skills show the need to understand a problem, analyze it, communicate about it, educate or persuade where necessary and work toward solutions.

  9. 9A firm has noticed declining customer satisfaction but has not yet worked out why, and wants to build a full plan to grow its consulting services around this issue over time. Apply the course's five-step practice consultancy development strategy, in order, to this situation, explaining what the firm should do at each step.

    • Step 1, define the current issues: the firm should conceptualize needs in the market and clearly identify and understand that declining customer satisfaction is the problem, since the notes give declining customer satisfaction as one of the example issues and state that issues should be clearly defined before solutions are developed.
    • Step 2, specify target consulting projects: the firm should evaluate the environment, understand the degree of organizational change involved, and select which specific consulting projects around this issue to target.
    • Step 3, recognize strengths and weaknesses: the firm should consider its own assumptions about its abilities and determine its pathway forward based on what it can realistically deliver.
    • Step 4, design the consultancy assignment: the firm should develop an action plan, agree with clients, understand the organizational change involved, and match the project design with client needs.
    • Step 5, implementation and monitoring: the firm should take action, involve the client, and understand hidden factors while the work is carried out.

  10. 10Explain, using the course's own points on the meaning and nature of consultancy management, what makes consultancy "knowledge-based" and "professional," why the relationship between a consultant and a client is described as temporary, and why confidentiality matters given that consultancy is meant to provide independent, objective advice.

    The notes describe consultancy as the professional practice of providing expert advice to organizations or individuals to improve performance and solve problems, and state that its nature is knowledge-based and professional; this is what makes it "knowledge-based," since the value being provided is expert analysis and specialized knowledge rather than routine labor, and the notes add that consultancy creates organizational value through expert analysis and recommendations. The relationship is described as temporary because a consultant is normally brought in to diagnose a problem and recommend solutions for a particular need, connected in the notes with decision-making, rather than becoming a permanent part of the client organization; once the assignment and its recommendations are complete, the engagement ends. Confidentiality and professionalism are required, according to the notes, and this matters because consultancy is meant to provide objective, independent and specialized knowledge; a consultant who does not protect confidential client information could not be trusted with the sensitive details needed to investigate a problem properly, which would undermine the independence and objectivity that the whole service depends on.

  11. 11An organization asks a consultant to analyze why its programs are underperforming, recommend strategic changes, and also review whether its project results are being properly tracked and assessed. Using the course's list of main functions of consultancy and its list of types of consultancy, identify which functions are involved and which type or types of consultancy this assignment falls under, with reasons.

    Three main functions from the notes are involved: analyzing organizational problems, since the consultant must work out why the programs are underperforming; providing strategic advice, since strategic changes are being recommended; and conducting monitoring and evaluation activities, since the consultant is reviewing whether project results are properly tracked and assessed. On types of consultancy, the analysis of underperformance and strategic recommendations fall under management consultancy, since this is the general type concerned with organizational problems and strategic advice, while the review of tracking and assessment of project results specifically falls under Monitoring and Evaluation consultancy, which the notes list as its own distinct type. The assignment therefore combines management consultancy and Monitoring and Evaluation consultancy rather than fitting only one type.

  12. 12A consultancy firm is operating in a period where clients are more value-conscious and compare costs closely, where artificial intelligence and data analytics are changing how consulting work is delivered, and where the firm is finding it hard to keep skilled staff. Using the course's lists of consultancy market conditions and market challenges, analyze this situation and recommend, using only ideas already in the notes, how the firm should respond.

    The scenario reflects several market conditions named in the notes: increased competition, described as clients being more value-conscious, comparing costs and expecting measurable results; and technology disruption, described as AI and data analytics redefining consulting services, with firms increasingly using technology to augment insights and automate repeatable tasks (Gartner, 2024). The difficulty retaining skilled staff matches one of the listed market challenges directly, difficulty maintaining skilled professionals, alongside the related challenge of high competition among firms. Based only on what the notes state, the firm's response should be to use technology to augment insights and automate repeatable tasks, as the notes suggest, so it can deliver clearly measurable results that answer value-conscious clients' cost comparisons, while also recognizing that retaining skilled professionals is a widely faced challenge in the market rather than a problem unique to the firm, which should shape how seriously it invests in keeping its people.

  13. 13A person wants to establish a new consultancy firm from scratch. Using the course's procedure for establishing a consultancy firm, list the steps in the correct order and explain, for this person's situation, what each step involves.

    1. Identify an area of specialization: the person must decide what specific field or industry the firm will focus on before anything else.
    2. Conduct market research: the person must study demand and conditions for that specialization.
    3. Prepare a business plan: the person must set out how the firm will operate and grow.
    4. Register the business legally: the person must complete formal legal registration of the business.
    5. Obtain licenses and tax registration: the person must secure the necessary licenses and register for tax.
    6. Develop marketing strategies: the person must plan how the firm will attract clients.
    7. Register with professional agencies: the person must register with the relevant professional bodies, and the notes give NEST as an example of such an agency.

  14. 14During an M&E assignment, a consultant must collect accurate data from confused stakeholders, manage stakeholder expectations that differ from what the project can deliver, and later present the findings clearly. Using the course's interpersonal communication skills and its table on communication skills in M&E consulting, identify which specific skills are needed for each of these three tasks and explain what each skill contributes.

    For collecting accurate data from confused stakeholders, the consultant needs active listening, which the notes say improves stakeholder understanding, together with interview skills, which the notes say support accurate data collection. For managing stakeholder expectations, the consultant needs negotiation skills, which the notes say help manage stakeholder expectations, and this connects to the broader interpersonal communication skill of negotiation and persuasion and to conflict resolution listed among the course's interpersonal communication skills. For presenting the findings clearly, the consultant needs presentation skills, which the notes say help explain findings clearly, supported by the broader skill of presentation and report writing. Across all three tasks, the notes also note that good communication in general strengthens trust and collaboration, which supports the whole engagement, and that these skills as a whole support strong client relationships, understanding client needs, presenting solutions and fostering trust and collaboration.

  15. 15A newly registered consultancy firm in Tanzania wants to become fully tax compliant. Using the course's Tanzania-specific tax notes, explain the steps the firm should take, including registration and the different returns it must file, and then use the course's list of reasons why tax compliance matters to justify why this effort is worthwhile for the firm.

    According to the Tanzania-specific notes, the firm should first register with the Tanzania Revenue Authority (TRA) to obtain a Taxpayer Identification Number (TIN), and then understand its obligations, which include income tax, VAT and withholding tax (WHT). It must then file returns and keep records as stated in the notes: monthly VAT returns, quarterly provisional income tax returns, and annual income tax returns. Because of the regulatory complexity involved and to reduce audit risks, the notes advise the firm to seek licensed tax practitioners or accountants, and they warn that failure to comply may result in penalties, interest charges or audits. This effort is worthwhile because the notes list several benefits of tax compliance: it prevents legal penalties and fines, improves organizational credibility, builds trust with clients and government authorities, supports financial transparency, and helps firms qualify for contracts and tenders, all of which directly benefit a newly registered firm trying to establish itself in the market.
