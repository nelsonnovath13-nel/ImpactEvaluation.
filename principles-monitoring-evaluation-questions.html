Practice Questions

15 challenging questions covering this course, designed to test the kind of thinking a real exam would demand. Attempt every question fully before you look at the model answers.

View Model Answers →
How to use this: Work through all 15 questions on your own first, in writing if possible. Then open the Model Answers page to check your understanding.

  1. A district health office says it is "monitoring" its malaria programme because it receives monthly activity reports from each clinic. Using the distinctions the notes draw between monitoring, reporting, evaluation and impact evaluation, explain why receiving reports is not the same as monitoring, and state what the office would additionally need to do to be conducting impact evaluation.

  2. A youth employment project collects data on training attendance, but it never asks trainees how they feel about the training or whether they trust the process, and it never checks whether the project is following its donor's contractual requirements. Using the six types of monitoring described in the notes, name two types of monitoring that are missing here and explain what each would add.

  3. A water project delivered all its planned wells on budget and on schedule, and beneficiaries said they were satisfied at the time. Two years after the project closed, however, half the wells are no longer working because no plan was made for spare parts or local maintenance capacity. Using the evaluation criteria in the notes, identify which criterion is most clearly failing here, and explain why efficiency and effectiveness alone do not capture this failure.

  4. An NGO has a costed M&E work plan, trained M&E staff, functioning databases and regular data audits in place. However, senior managers admit they have never changed a programme decision because of M&E findings. Using the three linked rings of the 12-component M&E system, diagnose what is missing and explain why the notes would describe this as the system "not being used for its central purpose."

  5. A donor insists that the mid-term review of a livelihoods project be led entirely by external consultants using pre-set indicators, with no involvement of the community members whose livelihoods are being assessed. Which M&E approach does this reflect? Using the notes' comparison of approaches, explain two specific ways a participatory approach would change how the review is conducted.

  6. An education project sets a target of raising the primary completion rate from 40% to 65% within four years, but no data on completion rates was collected before the project began. Using the distinct roles of baseline, target and indicator described in the notes, explain the problem this creates and why having a target alone is not enough.

  7. A programme wants to understand not just whether farmers adopted a new technique, but the personal reasons behind a few particularly striking individual transformations, to use as evidence for donors. Which method named in the notes best fits this need, and why would a structured survey be less suitable for this particular purpose?

  8. Using the course's own youth-unemployment example (skills training leading to improved employability, employment and reduced poverty), explain what belongs in the Theory of Change and what belongs in the Theory of Action, and explain how monitoring and evaluation each make use of these two ideas differently.

  9. A rural sanitation project trains 500 households in latrine construction, which designers expect will lead to increased latrine usage and, ultimately, reduced diarrheal disease rates in the region. Using LFA's four levels and its vertical "If–Then" logic, arrange these elements into the correct LFA hierarchy from Activities up to Goal/Impact. Then explain the role that Assumptions/Risks play in connecting one level to the next.

  10. A project distributes free school meals and observes that attendance improves in the same term. Using the core components of the causal analysis framework, explain what would still need to be established before concluding that the meals caused the improvement, and name at least two of the causal validation approaches listed in the notes that could be used to test this.

  11. A construction project manager wants to know whether the concrete supplier is meeting quality standards, whether the schedule is slipping and whether the budget is on track, but has not yet defined KPIs or a monitoring plan. Using the nine detailed steps of project monitoring in the notes, identify which step must come first, and explain why skipping it undermines the later step of comparing actual against planned performance.

  12. A school-building project team reviews weekly progress reports during construction to adjust schedules and resource allocation. Separately, after the school opens, an external team studies whether student enrollment increased. Identify which evaluation type from the notes each activity represents, and explain the difference in purpose and timing between the two.

  13. An M&E officer suspects a partner organization has been submitting inflated beneficiary numbers. Using the key aspects of data auditing listed in the notes, describe three specific checks the officer could perform to investigate this concern.

  14. A researcher needs to explore sensitive personal experiences as part of a programme evaluation. Using the notes' descriptions of semi-structured interviews and focus groups, explain why a semi-structured interview would usually be more appropriate than a focus group for this purpose, referring to how each method is structured.

  15. Explain how the Logical Framework's vertical "If–Then" logic and the causal analysis framework's causal chain (Inputs → Activities → Outputs → Outcomes → Impact) are similar. Then explain what causal analysis adds that the LFA alone does not, referring to the causal validation approaches named in the notes.
