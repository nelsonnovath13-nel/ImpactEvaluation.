Practice Questions

15 challenging questions covering this course, designed to test the kind of thinking a real exam would demand. Attempt every question fully before you look at the model answers.

View Model Answers →
How to use this: Work through all 15 questions on your own first, in writing if possible. Then open the Model Answers page to check your understanding.

  1. A community health project currently has a small grant, several trained volunteers and used equipment, but no plan for what happens when the grant ends. Using the three fundamental sustainability streams named in the course notes, explain why this project might fail even if it has "sufficient funds" today, and describe what each stream would require the project to do.

  2. A district wants to mobilize community resources to build a borehole, but local leaders disagree on where it should be sited. Using the "Steps to Successful Community Resource Mobilization" from the course notes, describe the sequence the project team should follow before beginning to mobilize resources, and explain why starting with a transparent community selection process matters for this scenario.

  3. A toll road project needs financing for both construction and long-term maintenance. Compare the user-pay mechanism and the cost-sharing mechanism as described in the course notes: define each, give one example of each from the notes, and use the notes' comparison table (source of funds, timing, main objective) to explain which mechanism is the better fit for financing a toll road, and why.

  4. An NGO signs a written agreement with a donor stating that the NGO must contribute 20% of a project's cost as a condition of receiving the grant. A separate community partner verbally promises to also contribute labor, but this promise is never written down anywhere. Using the three types of cost sharing named in the notes (mandatory, voluntary committed, voluntary uncommitted), classify each contribution and justify your classification.

  5. A government wants a private company to finance, design, build and operate a new toll bridge, collecting user fees to recover its investment, after which the bridge will be transferred back to the government after a fixed period. Which PPP delivery model listed in the course notes best matches this arrangement — BOOT, BOO or DBFO? Define all three and justify your choice using their distinguishing features.

  6. A private partner completes a new highway PPP on schedule and on budget, but only about half as many drivers use it as forecast, leaving the company unable to recover its investment through tolls. In a separate case, a construction firm faces major cost overruns and delays during the building phase of a rail PPP. Using the risk categories named in the course notes, identify which named risk affects each project and explain what each risk category means.

  7. A company's current ratio looks healthy, but a financial analyst warns that this may be misleading because a large share of its current assets is tied up in slow-moving inventory. Using the formulas for the current ratio, quick ratio and cash ratio given in the notes, explain how each ratio differs in what it includes, and explain why the quick ratio or cash ratio might give a more cautious picture of this company's liquidity than the current ratio does.

  8. The course notes' D Company example shows return on equity declining from 0.22 in 2012 to 0.20 in 2013, even though return on assets rose from 0.11 to 0.13 over the same period. Using the DuPont relationship "Return on equity = (Total assets ÷ Shareholders' equity) × Return on assets" and the financial leverage figures given in the notes (2.00 in 2012, falling to 1.60 in 2013), show the arithmetic that explains why ROE fell despite ROA rising.

  9. The course notes' shareholder example produces a book value per share of $1.00, a price-to-earnings (P/E) ratio of 16.67, dividends per share (DPS) of $0.12, a dividend payout ratio of 40% and a plowback ratio of 60%. Define the dividend payout ratio and the plowback ratio using the formulas in the notes, show how the 60% plowback figure is derived from the 40% payout figure, and explain in plain terms what a 40% payout / 60% plowback combination tells you about how this company is using its earnings.

  10. A company's inventory turnover is low and its receivables turnover is also low, meaning both inventory and receivables are held for a long time before converting to cash. Using the formulas for days inventory, days receivables and the operating cycle given in the notes, explain what a "long operating cycle" means for a company's liquidity needs, and explain how the net operating cycle formula differs from the operating cycle formula by incorporating days payables.

  11. A project finance loan for a data-center construction project is structured on a non-recourse basis. At the midpoint of the schedule, the server installation task is only 50% complete when the project schedule expected it to be 100% complete. (a) Using the notes' description of a non-recourse loan, explain what this financing structure means for the lender if the project fails. (b) Using the rate of performance formula (RP = actual work completed ÷ percentage of work planned), calculate the rate of performance for the server installation task and explain what this figure indicates about the project's progress.

  12. A new NGO department head wants to build next year's budget quickly by adjusting last year's actual spending figures for expected inflation and volume changes, since the department's activities won't change dramatically. Which of the three project/programmatic budgeting approaches named in the notes — zero-based, top-down or incremental — best fits this situation? Define all three, and explain one advantage and one disadvantage of the chosen approach as given in the notes.

  13. The course notes' wraparound example uses $198,540 in revenue collected from 3,948 eligible visits, measured against a $100.00 per-visit rate used for cost-based/managed-care comparison. (a) Calculate the revenue per visit. (b) Calculate the shortfall per visit compared to the $100.00 rate. (c) Explain what a "wraparound" figure represents in this context, given that the projected wraparound in the notes is $196,260.

  14. An NGO in Arusha needs 50 desks for a community learning center at an estimated cost of TZS 15 million. The notes describe a process of writing specifications, obtaining at least three qualified quotations, evaluating by price, quality, delivery and warranty, awarding based on best overall value, and inspecting before payment. Identify at least four of the Ten Principles of Public Procurement that this process demonstrates, and explain how each specific action in the example demonstrates that principle.

  15. A donor-funded NGO suddenly needs a specific piece of medical equipment within 48 hours because of an emergency, and only one authorized supplier in the country carries that exact model. Using the Common Methods table in the notes (Petty Cash Purchase, Request for Quotations, Open Tender, Restricted Tender, Single-Source Procurement), identify which method is justified here and explain why the other methods would not fit, referencing their typical use as described in the notes.
